A Research Note records the claim separately from its reach, its evidence, and what remains unknown. Read **Current finding** together with **What this research does not show** to understand the result without silently enlarging it.

To assess validity in more detail, continue to **Evidence boundary**, **UNKNOWN**, and **Falsification targets**. To obtain the same observation yourself, use **Reproduce** for the declared inputs and commands.

## How each note is ordered

1. **Current finding** — what can be claimed now.
2. **Key figure** — the one relationship at the centre of the claim.
3. **What this research shows / does not show** — where the claim reaches, and where it stops.
4. **Why this matters** — what changes because of this result.
5. **Research question, Method, Results** — what was actually done.
6. **What changed / What failed** — revisions to earlier claims, and predictions that missed.
7. **Evidence boundary / UNKNOWN / Falsification targets** — the limits to read carefully.
8. **Reproduce / Evidence / External audit** — where to start if you want to check it.

## Read the labels literally

- **Evidence class** says what was observed, not how important the topic is. → [[en/how-to-read/evidence-levels|Compare evidence classes]]
- **UNKNOWN** marks where the evidence stops and what observation would move it. It is not filler.
- **Editorial review** means the public wording and privacy boundary were checked. It does not mean scientific, domain-expert, or peer review.
- **Quick reproduction** checks public artifacts along a bounded path. It is not an independent replication.

## Read corrections forward

Dated notes stay part of the record. When later evidence narrows a claim, the old note carries a prominent forward link and the research index points at the current claim. Failed predictions, analysis defects, and withdrawn claims are not deleted.
