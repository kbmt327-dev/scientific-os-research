You do not need to read every section. Stop at the depth that answers your question.

<div class="depth-guide">
  <section><b>01 · DISCOVER</b><h3>What is the finding?</h3><p>Read the opening claim, status, and key figure. About 15 seconds.</p></section>
  <section><b>02 · UNDERSTAND</b><h3>Why, and under what conditions?</h3><p>Read why it matters, the mechanism, and Shows / Does not show.</p></section>
  <section><b>03 · INSPECT</b><h3>Would the evidence change your mind?</h3><p>Inspect predictions, failures, evidence boundary, artifacts, and `UNKNOWN`.</p></section>
  <section><b>04 · REPRODUCE</b><h3>Can you obtain the same observation?</h3><p>Run the declared package and report the commit, environment, and deviation.</p></section>
</div>

## Read the labels literally

- **Evidence class** says what was observed—not how important the topic is. [[evidence-levels|Compare evidence levels]].
- **UNKNOWN** is an evidence boundary and a request for a discriminating observation, not filler.
- **Editorial review** means the public wording and privacy boundary were checked. It does not mean scientific, domain-expert, or peer review.
- **Quick reproduction** checks public artifacts and bounded paths. It is not an independent replication.

## Read corrections forward

Dated notes remain part of the record. When later evidence narrows a claim, the old note carries a prominent forward link and the research index marks the current note. A failed prediction, analysis defect, or demoted claim remains visible.
