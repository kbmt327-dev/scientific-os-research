## Research question

Instead of choosing cipher families one at a time, write down a grammar of parts first (alphabets, rules that pick a chart row at each position, chart shapes), generate every procedure it allows, and test each against the 24 crib letters. Does any of them fit K4?

## Why this matters

Until this step each family was proposed by hand, so a negative result said as much about the proposer as about K4. A grammar fixed before running makes the searched space explicit and countable: what was not found is stated with its size, and what lies outside is listed.

## Method

- **Alphabets (274).** A–Z, the KRYPTOS alphabet, their reverses, and keyed alphabets from Kryptos words, the clue words and the 2025 theme words (Egypt, Berlin, the clock, the message), each forward and reversed.
- **Row selectors (16.6 million for a 26-row chart).** Linear in position; 114 position and ciphertext features (carved row and column, W segment, distance from the last W, counts of each earlier ciphertext letter, residues and quotients); periodic keywords (38,192); ciphertext autokey (lags 1–21); stride running keys over 14 Kryptos and World Clock texts (15.4 million); Carter & Mace vol. 1 (1.1 million).
- **Chart shapes.** Four K1–K3 variants (Quagmire-type shifts, Beaufort forms, keyword plus linear key) and six different methods: two stacked shifts on different alphabets (`TWO`, `TWOB`), affine rows whose multiplier changes with position (`AFF`), rows that are keyword alphabets of successive words, 26-letter slices of a text, or powers of one permutation. Transposition parts are left out, because the cribs are read at their carved positions.
- **Test.** A procedure passes if it maps all 24 crib letters correctly. With bijective rows this becomes "is the required key sequence in the selector family", answered by hash lookup; any hit is rebuilt and checked.
- **Controls.** Two planted procedures per shape, each found by searching its whole shape (18/18). Two shuffled K4s as nulls. The script was committed before it was run on K4.
- **Follow-ups on the same grammar.** A K1–K3-type shift mask stacked before or after each different-method chart (EP-0127); each crib judged alone, with features counted from the start of its segment (EP-0134); up to two or three wrong crib letters (EP-0131).

## Results

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole crib (EP-0119) | 4.7 × 10¹⁵ | **0** | 0, 0 | 18/18 | 5 × 10⁻¹⁹ |

| Up to 3 crib errors (EP-0131) | 4.7 × 10¹⁵ | **0** | 0, 0 | 9/9 and 9/9 | 1.6 × 10⁻¹¹ |

| Crib 2 alone, auxiliary (EP-0134) | 4.7 × 10¹⁵ | 4 identities, 0 informative | 0, 0 | 7/9 | 1.3 |

In the error runs the first control count plants errors on the plaintext side, the second a carving error. The per-crib hits are identities: the first layer keyed by the crib word on a reversed alphabet collapses the plaintext to a constant, and the second layer uses K4's own ciphertext as its running key.

## Current finding

No procedure in a grammar of 4.7 × 10¹⁵ fully specified procedures fits K4's cribs. Nearly all of them (99.9%) are different methods rather than one shifted chart. The result holds with a shift mask stacked on top, with each crib judged separately, and with up to three wrong crib letters.

## Key figure

![Horizontal bars of log2 procedures per chart shape, from 28.4 bits for row-gathering charts to 51.0 bits for two stacked shifts; all shapes together 52.1 bits, with a stacked mask 107.6 bits; a red line at 112.8 bits marks what the 24 crib letters can pin down](/assets/kryptos-k4-procedure-enumerator.svg)

The crib carries about 113 bits. The whole grammar uses 52 bits, so a false pass is effectively impossible and a true procedure in the grammar would stand out. With the stacked mask the space reaches 108 bits, close to the crib's limit; this shape cannot be widened further and still be decided.

## What this research shows

- The grammar, as written before the run, contains no procedure that fits K4, including its K1–K3-variant part (2.7 × 10¹²), which reproduces earlier rejections.
- "The Ws switch the procedure, so a whole-crib test missed it" does not hold inside the grammar: each crib alone gives no informative hit.
- "A carving error hides the procedure" does not hold for up to three errors, except for selectors that read the ciphertext, where one carving error changes later selector values (4 of 18 carving controls missed at two errors).

## What this research does not show

It does not show that K4 is rarer than random: shuffled ciphertexts also give 0, as the chance values predict. It says nothing about procedures outside the grammar: hand-made charts, homophones, codebooks, keys from sources not listed, three or more layers, or plaintext autokey.

## What changed

Families are no longer proposed one by one. The searched space is written down, counted and committed before running, and later extensions add parts with their counts.

## What failed

Nothing in the grammar fit. The carving-error branch of EP-0131 missed 4 of 18 planted errors at two errors, all in ciphertext-reading selectors, so that corner is covered only when an error does not spread.

## Evidence boundary

Public K4 ciphertext and cribs, and the author's own code. Parts of the grammar are built from the K1–K3 texts and the carved tableau; this site does not publish those, so the search itself is not rerunnable from the public package. The public check covers the counts and chance values.

## UNKNOWN

Which parts a real solution would need. Whether the key source is one of the texts used, or something not listed. External independent replications: zero.

## Falsification targets

A procedure inside this grammar that fits the 24 crib letters would overturn the result; so would a bug that makes the hash test miss planted procedures of some shape. Adding a new part and finding a fit would not overturn it, but would locate the answer outside the grammar as stated.
